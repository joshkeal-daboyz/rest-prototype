## Rules

### 16.11 Fire escapes (ticket 058)

`escapes.ts` holds the measured numbers (`ESCAPE_GEOMETRY`), the pure placement and the check, `dressing.ts` places the escape of a block (16.5 says which blocks get one), and `models3d.ts` draws it.

- **Rule [D-058b]**: every fire escape, on the tile city and on the ladder maps, reads as one connected steel stair with a platform at each storey that a person can step out onto from a window.
- **Default (058)**: one platform per storey. Every floor over the base gets exactly one, the top floor included. A platform's deck is at that floor's measured window sill (`WINDOW_SILL_M`) less `ESCAPE_DECK_BELOW_SILL_M`, within `ESCAPE_DECK_TOL_M`: a person steps out over the sill onto a deck no more than a hand's height lower. A floor piece's sill is the lowest point of the window openings on its front (the glass of its lowest window), one number per storey (three on a `Stack` piece). A piece with no measured sill takes its style's median (`sillOf`).
- **Default (058)**: one continuous run. The first piece (the tall landing, with the drop ladder), a middle piece for each storey between, and the top piece. Pieces are placed by their measured deck and flight heights (`ESCAPE_GEOMETRY`), never by their bounds. A flight rises exactly one storey, from the surface of its own deck to the surface of the deck above, so a piece is scaled in y about its base until its flight's top meets the deck above within `ESCAPE_JOINT_TOL_M`, and shifted in x until the flight arrives at the hatch in that deck. The flights then stand one over the other.
- **Default (058)**: the drop ladder reaches the walk. Boxes run from the walk (y 0 of the lot frame) up to the pack ladder's foot: two rails `LADDER_RAIL_M` thick and rungs `LADDER_RUNG_M` thick every `LADDER_RUNG_PITCH_M`, in the escape's dark steel, within `ESCAPE_GROUND_TOL_M` of the walk. A real drop ladder hangs about 2.5 m up; this one touches down, because a ladder that ends in the air reads as a gap.
- **Default (058)**: when the pack's piece does not fit. A piece is scaled in y only within `ESCAPE_SCALE_Y` (steeper or shallower stairs still read as stairs). Outside that range, or when any of the three pieces is missing from the manifest or flagged scale-suspect, the escape is drawn in code (`codeEscape`): a deck of three slabs round a hatch, a rail and four posts, a flight of two stringers with treads between decks, and the ladder, as one piece with the key `CODE_ESCAPE_KEY` and the sizes of `CODE_ESCAPE`. A style is never skipped, so R7's count stays. With nothing loaded at all (no City Pack) there is no dressing, escapes included.
- **Default (061)**: eligibility: an apartment block of at least `ESCAPE_MIN_BAYS` columns and two floors, a mid column that is not a door or corner column and has a roof piece. Which blocks get one is not a per-block chance (see the next rules).
- **Rule [D-061a]**: the share of fire escapes is guaranteed, not left to chance.
- **Default [D-061b]**: how many. For each map, exactly `ceil(share x qualifying)` blocks get an escape. `qualifying` is R7's count (`citymetrics.ts`: an apartment style with at least `ESCAPE_MIN_BAYS` columns and two floors over the base). `share` is the map's R7 floor, which the caller passes: `ESCAPE_SHARE_LADDER` (0.5, `CITY_CHECK.R7`) on the ladder, `TILE_CITY_R7_MIN` (0.4) on the tile city (`cityApplyOptions`, `ApplyOptions.escapeShare`, `dressTown`'s `escapeShare`). The count never depends on a hash key or on how many blocks there are, only on the qualifying count. The share is one constant each.
- **Default (061)**: which blocks. `escapeBlocks` (`escapes.ts`, pure) runs first in `dressTown`, on the blocks that can host one (the eligibility above, and the escape fits its storeys and the kerb: a probe of `dressBlock` that stops after the escape). They are ranked by `stableHash(lot.key + "escape")`; escapes go in rank order, skipping a block when the block before or after it in its run already has one (so no two neighbouring blocks of a run both have one; a block that cannot host breaks the run). If that stops short of the target, each run that holds fewer than ceil(k / 2) of its k hostable blocks in a row is re-laid alternating (first, third, fifth, from its better-ranked end), best-ranked run first, until the target is met; a surplus is trimmed from the worst-ranked end. The target can always be met at a share of 0.5 or less: k hostable blocks in a row hold ceil(k / 2), and those sum to at least ceil(qualifying / 2) when every qualifying block can host.
- **Default (061)**: a shortfall is reported, not hidden. If fewer blocks can host than the target needs, every hostable block the neighbour rule allows gets one and `escapeBlocks` returns the rest as `escapeShort`; the tests name it and R7's check fails on such a map.
- **Default (061)**: chosen escapes are placed before the rest. A chosen block's escape takes its column's room first against `DRESS_PER_BAY_MAX` (it still joins the block's pieces where it did), and a chosen block keeps its escape past `DRESS_MAX` (the cap is on the rest of the dressing). A block dressed alone (`dressStack`) counts as chosen.
- **Default (058)**: the column cap counts an escape by the pieces the dressing used before the one-platform-per-storey rule. A platform on every storey (a piece each) and every other piece of dressing unchanged cannot both hold under the cap of `DRESS_PER_BAY_MAX` pieces per column: a block of four or five storeys has one piece more than stacking by bounds used, and the column's small roof props would be dropped to make room. So against the cap an escape counts the old pieces (the tall landing, a middle piece while it and the top still fit under the roof, the top piece), and nothing else on the block moves. The cost is that the escape column of a four or five storey block draws 7 pieces where `DRESS_PER_BAY_MAX` is 6. A code-drawn escape counts as one.
- **Default (058)**: Apartment_03 has a wide central window and two narrow side windows with higher sills. The deck is under the lowest, the central window's sill, so a person still climbs out of the side windows and the slab never crosses the bottom of the style's biggest window.
`escapeCheck` (pure, run by `escapes.test.ts` on the default tile city, seeds 1 to 10 at 3 x 3, and the three maps of 16.8) reports per escape the worst deck-to-sill offset, the worst flight joint (a flight top's height from the deck above and its x from that deck's hatch), the height of the ladder's foot above the walk, and the storeys with no platform. It passes when none is missing, every deck is within `ESCAPE_DECK_TOL_M`, every joint within `ESCAPE_JOINT_TOL_M` and the foot within `ESCAPE_GROUND_TOL_M`. The code-drawn boxes are one InstancedMesh for the whole town, hung under the first landing's mesh in the dressing group.
