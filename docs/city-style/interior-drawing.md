## Rules

### 19.8 What the drawing does (ticket 057 D)

This part describes the old path's drawing (`tilecity3d.ts`, `tileslots.ts` and `kerbline.ts`, at `city=tiles-old` until L2). On the pipeline `plan3d.ts` draws the plan's layers (stage 8's ground, stage 4's kerb line in `src/city/edge/kerbline.ts`) and decides nothing about them.

- **Default (057)**: `tilecity3d.ts` draws every interior role from `interiorItems` (`tileslots.ts`), one `InstancedMesh` per key and sub-mesh, in a group of its own beside the street tiles', with a code-drawn fallback per kind of surface when its key is absent (CI and a fresh checkout). Interior ground is at level 0, flush with the pavement's walk. It reads `TileCity.interior`, `courts`, `passages`, `greenProps` and `alleyBins` and nothing else, and takes no input. Props (the trees and the bench of the greens and the bins of the alleys) are optional dressing, pack pieces only, each chosen by a hash of the city's seed and its point, so the same city gives the same props. A prop whose piece is missing, or lost to the draw-call cap, is not drawn.
- **Default (063)**: the interior paving is one plain slab. `INTERIOR_KEYS` gives the apron, the paths, the park edge and the ring-2 strip the one key, `SM_Env_Sidewalk_01` (opaque, only the walk's slab and joint colours), and `PASSAGE_FLOOR_KEYS` is the same key. The strip and the floors are the same tiles as the apron, with the code quad as their fallback only. The draw is one InstancedMesh for all of them, so the interiors take no more draw calls than before.
- **Default (063)**: the inlets are paved. A residents' court's drive (`TileCourt.drive`, the two ring-1 cells of a court that opens on a street) and the first and last cell of an alley's lane are `SM_Env_Sidewalk_01` at the walk, from the kerb cut to where the opening meets the interior, in place of the bare road (`inletCells` in `tileslots.ts`). The court's first row of stalls (ring 2) keeps the bare road and `ParkingLines_01`, the alley lane beyond ring 1 keeps the bare road, and a frontage lot of `TileCity.frontLots` and an alley block's own court (whose drive is beside the lane, not through a row) are not inlets and keep their asphalt. The kerb cut (`Sidewalk_Dip_01`) on the pavement band is unchanged, so the dropped kerb meets paving. `TileCity` is not changed: the street layer draws the drives (a `lotSurface` cell whose key is the slab when it is loaded, the bare road when it is not) and the interiors draw the alley mouths.
- **Default (063)**: the kerb line (`kerbLine` in `kerbline.ts`, pure) is one path per stretch of walk of each street side, from corner to corner, on the cell edges: the pavement band's carriageway edge; a flank at each end of a build-out stretch and the build-out's outer edge; and at each inlet (a kerb cut: a court drive or a frontage lot of `TileCity.frontLots`, 2 cells, or an alley's mouth, 1 cell; a gated passage has none) a return up the near side of the opening to the building line, the opening (no kerb), the return down the far side, and the dipped mouth (`Sidewalk_Dip_01`) between the two. `tileKerbPlan` is what the drawing puts on it, from the finished city: each pack piece's own face at its measured inset (`KERB_FACES_M`), and the code strips. Every face is on the line within `KERB_TOL_M` in plan and `KERB_HEIGHT_TOL_M` in lip height, and no part of the line is uncovered for over `KERB_GAP_MAX_M`; a fallback strip for a pavement or build-out cell drawn in code stands on the same inset line (`KERB_INSET_M`), at the same lip height, so fallback and pack agree.
  - The cells (`tilecity.ts` lays them as bits of a pavement cell's `variant`, never on a grate): `PAVEMENT_RETURN_NEAR` on the pavement cell just before an inlet's first kerb-cut cell along its street and `PAVEMENT_RETURN_FAR` on the one just after its last, and `PAVEMENT_FLUSH` on a cell with a build-out in front of it. The return cell is the plain straight, with its own street kerb, and a code strip down its side edge from the street kerb face to the building line. `TILE_SLOTS` is unchanged. A return cell and a flush cell are never a grate, so the 1-in-6 grates are a little fewer; the pole slots are unchanged.
  - The returns are the straight and a strip, not `Sidewalk_Dip_Corner_01` (a ramp, see chapter 10), and the strip is squared at the corner, not rounded: the pack's lip along the street edge runs on to the cell's edge and cannot be carved, so a rounded corner would stand beside a square one. The strip stands behind its face, on the walk side.
  - One more InstancedMesh, `tile:kerb-strips`, holds every code strip (a city drawn wholly from the pack has them too, so it is not named as a cell's fallback); it counts in `TILE_DRAW_CALLS_MAX`. The slab's mesh also holds the flush cells.
- **Default (057) [D-057q]**: draw calls. The interiors have their own cap, `INTERIOR_DRAW_CALLS_MAX`, on top of `TILE_DRAW_CALLS_MAX` for the street's tiles: if the usable keys would take more, the keys with the fewest instances are dropped first. The dev view logs a tile-city-interiors line once its models are applied (what stands inside the blocks, from the pack and in code, and the draw calls).
- **Default (057)**: furniture. A lot that became a court is no longer dressed as a frontage lot (`courtLotPieces` in `tilecity3d.ts`): the town still holds each court's mouth as a 10 m parking lot, so `furnitureLayout` would put a second lines tile 5 mm under the interiors' own and barriers across the near stalls' opening. The drive is a kerb cut and a lot surface only. A block in `TileCity.frontLots` keeps its frontage lot's furniture.
- **Default (057)**: `&sheet=1` shows each new piece once, labelled with the interior roles it serves.

`puzzle=09-shrine-circuit&view=3d` is unaffected: the interiors, passages and the explicit alignment are the tile view's, and a puzzle map's `packTown` call is as it was (`packpin.test.ts`).
