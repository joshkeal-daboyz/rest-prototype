## What the ticket asked for, as I understood it
Add a time-based battery to `src/grid/`. It charges from the feed at 1/s up to `capacity`. When released, it powers its far side for `charge` seconds and doesn't count toward the feed's load. Add relay switches, which can only be thrown while current reaches them and keep their position afterwards. Time enters only through `advance(dt)`, and maps without these pieces behave exactly as before. The stated purpose: the battery's window is "for *setting* the section, not keeping it lit".

### The builder's rule interpretations
1. **Time is not undoable** (undo leaves charge and released alone; `release` isn't in history and isn't an action). **Reasonable gap-fill.** The ticket's "Charge is kept across undo/reset as the file's `charge` value" is ambiguous. Reading it as "undo keeps charge, reset restores the file value" is defensible, and the test "charge starts at the file's value…, reset returns to it, and undo leaves charge alone" pins it down. Two side effects to know about (see Could improve 1–2): undo can move a relay that is now dead, and releases don't show in `actions`.
2. **The feed's current never passes through a battery.** **Matches the ticket's intent.** "a section the feed can't reach" and "counts toward the feed's capacity only if the feed also reaches it" only make sense if the battery blocks. If it passed current through, its far side would be fed whenever it charges. "sits on the grid like a junction" refers to its position, not to conducting. The schema row is silent, so this is a gap-fill consistent with both documents. A battery is now a new non-conducting element that hides its far side's faults from the feed. That's correct, but worth a line in SCHEMA.md (Could improve 4).
3. **A battery charges from a released battery too.** **Reasonable reading of the schema's "live power reaches it through input", but it has a flaw.** One released battery charges every downstream battery at 1/s each, so charge multiplies. Probe: A (charge 4) released into B and C (both 0) leaves B = 4, C = 4, so 4 units became 8. No acceptance criterion forbids it, so it goes under Could improve 3, but it needs a decision before a puzzle chains batteries.
4. **Fuses and brownout are judged on the feed's flow only.** **Matches.** The ticket's open question cuts a released battery as "regardless of load (it's a time limit, not a load limit)", and the schema row repeats that. A fuse on a battery-only far side never blows on battery power. That follows from the same cut.
5. **A flat battery immediately starts recharging if the feed reaches it.** **Matches.** Both ticket and schema say it charges whenever live power reaches its input and it is not released; nothing says a flat battery waits. The test "release again stops the battery early…" also covers recharging after an early stop.

## Rule conflicts and risks
- **A job can be won on battery power alone, and then the grid freezes.** `complete` uses the merged `lit`, so a target on the battery's far side counts as lit while the battery is released, and `job:completed` fires. The builder asserts this on purpose ("a battery-only section can win the job"). `finished` then locks out `throwSwitch`, `undo`, `release` and repairs, while `advance` keeps draining. Probe: release, the target is lit and the job completes; 2 s later `complete` is false, the target is dark, and every input returns false. So the Relight is declared on a lamp that goes out seconds later. This contradicts the ticket's stated design, "the window is for *setting* the section, not keeping it lit". In the intended layout (battery, then a relay section, then the targets the feed will later reach), the player wins just by clicking the battery and never solves the relay section. → Must fix 1.
- Undo bypasses the relay rule backwards. Throw a relay on battery power, let the battery go flat, then `undo`: the relay flips back while nothing reaches it (probe: `undo()` returns true, `sw` goes 1 to 0, `powered.has("sw")` is false). This gives the player nothing they didn't have before, but it is a relay changing state while dead.
- Charge multiplication through fan-out (interpretation 3 above).
- No purity, folder or dependency problems: `grid.ts` imports only `../core`, there are no timers or DOM, and nothing is touched outside `src/grid/`. `node:fs` appears only in the test file. The 3D game can take `src/grid/` as it is. A frame loop calling `advance(dt)` is the whole integration.
- The `advance` loop guard (10 000 sub-steps) is fine; there are at most two events per battery per step.

## Must fix
1. **`src/grid/grid.ts` (`settle`, the `complete` field) and `src/grid/battery.test.ts`:** a target lit only by a released battery must not complete the job. Compute `complete` from lamps lit by the feed's own flow (`f.powered`), with the same brownout check. Battery-lit lamps stay in `lit`, since they are lit for reading and rendering. Replace the test "a battery-only section can win the job" with one named for the rule: e.g. "a target lit only by a released battery does not complete the job; it completes once the feed reaches it". Without this, the intended battery-and-relay puzzle can be skipped by clicking the battery once, against the ticket's own design paragraph. If Josh rules instead that battery light should win, this item drops; it is his call, but the branch shouldn't decide it silently.

## Could improve
1. Undo of a relay throw while the relay is dead. Either refuse it (`undo` returns false when the step it reverts was a relay throw and that relay isn't powered now), or record in SCHEMA.md that undo may move a dead relay. Josh's call; a sentence in the ticket is enough.
2. `release` doesn't increment `actions`. If `actions` is ever shown or scored, releases are player clicks and arguably should count. Fine to leave, but say so in SCHEMA.md.
3. Charging from a released battery. Either forbid it (charge only from the feed's own flow: `f.powered.has(bid)` instead of the merged `powered`), or drain the source by the number of batteries it charges. The first is one line and closes the multiplication.
4. Add the derived battery rules to SCHEMA.md "Rules the simulator derives": the feed never passes through a battery; a released battery's far side is free of fuse and brownout checks; flat means recharge if live. This is the lead-architect's file, so raise it in the ticket rather than on this branch.
5. `release` returns false after `finished`, even to stop a running release early; `advance` keeps draining after the job is done. Harmless today. With Must fix 1 applied, it only matters if the feed completes the job while a battery is still released.
