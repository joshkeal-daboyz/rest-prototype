## Does the branch do it?

- **Beat recipe:** `recipes.ts` has `beatThump()`: a sine on `noteHz("A1")` at −28 dB, attack 0.01, release 0.3. The tick is a sine on `noteHz("A2")` at −42 dB. - **Scheduler:** `BeatScheduler.collect` in `layers.ts` only asks `clock.between(t, t+horizon)`, so a past beat is never returned. It remembers the last beat handed out, so overlapping frames don't repeat one. `on=false` resets it, and so does a clock going backwards. It never reads a clock: the test greps layers.ts for `Date.now|performance.now|Math.random`. Tests cover overlap, the stall skip, off/on resume and `reset()`. Met. **Gap replay:** the scheduler can't replay after a gap, because it only ever looks at `[t, t+0.1)`. The 600 s test (`beat.test.ts`, "a 600 s gap … resyncs") does show at most one thump, then ≤3 over the next 120 frames. In that test the window happens to hold no beat, so it passes with 0 thumps. The code is what guarantees no replay, not this test.
- **Engine:** `engine.ts` `applyLayers` schedules `now + (b − timeSeconds)`. It only does so after the `ready()/ctx/player` check, so no context or a locked one schedules nothing. An undefined or non-finite time resets the scheduler and schedules nothing. `silence()` resets it. Tests on the fake context cover: the start time within 1 ms, once per beat over 600 frames, none when unpowered, none with no time, none when locked, and detach stopping pending thumps (stops 1→2) and resetting the scheduler. They also cover mute ramping the master to 0, and the path voice gain → duck → master, which is the same duck the hum uses and which the Relight dips. Met.

## Rule conflicts and risks
- `src/grid/` and `src/night/` aren't touched, no GDD rule changed, there is no new dependency, and there is no `Math.random`.
- Files outside the ticket's list: `src/audio/tuningShell.test.ts` was edited. It had built the choir through `levelDoneSound()`, so with the choir gone it had to change. It now feeds a hand-made "sung" voice straight to `Player`, which keeps 031's detune/formant/vibrato shell support tested, as the ticket wants. Two assertions were dropped from its vibrato test (Could improve 3). `notSoftened.test.ts` and its snapshot are new, which the "Not softened" criterion requires. Neither is a rule break.
- The ticket says "the drone becomes a beat", but the continuous hum (`HUM_DB`, `HUM_HZ`, held in the shell) still plays under the thumps. The ticket's text doesn't ask for it to go, and the not-softened pin keeps `HUM_DB`. So the build follows the ticket, but Josh may have expected the drone to *be replaced*. That is for his ears (see below).
- The audio lands about `ctx.outputLatency` later than the pulses: the thump is scheduled at `currentTime + (beatT − t)` with no latency compensation. On wired output that is a few ms. On Bluetooth it can be 100–200 ms, which would look like the chevrons leading the thump.
- The beat is scheduled up to 0.1 s ahead, so a thump already queued can still sound just after the last feed is cut (one thump at most). This is by design (`BEAT_HORIZON`).

### What only ears or eyes can judge (Josh)
1. Whether the A1 thump at −28 dB is audible and soft. On laptop speakers it may be all tick. Whether the A2 tick at −42 dB is enough to carry it.
2. Whether the hum under the beat should stay, or whether the beat was meant to replace the drone (a one-number change, `HUM_DB`, but a new ticket since 032 pinned it).
3. Whether 66 BPM feels calm, and whether a thump every 0.91 s gets tiresome over a long puzzle.
4. Whether the beat ducks naturally under the Relight and cues.
5. Whether the fifth (−30 dB) makes the chimes "pleasing" or muddy against the bed and the close's A3/E4. An octave is `CHIME_PARTNER_SEMITONES = 12`.
6. Whether the level-done cue sounds like the 025 one he approved ("good enough for now"). The pitches moved from 440 to 432, about −32 cents.
7. That switches, breakers, fuse pops and battery sounds are unchanged to the ear. The tests prove the recipes are identical.
8. Eyes: whether the chevrons' surge-and-settle reads as "pulsing to the beat". The old pulses flowed steadily at 90 px/s, and now they move one spacing per beat and mostly rest. Whether that feels slower or "stuck" between beats.
9. Eyes and ears together: whether pulses and thump look in sync on his output device (latency, above). Also whether they stay in step after a tab switch: the code resyncs on the next beat and never replays.
10. Whether the pulses still beat when muted or before the first click. This is pure in `t`, so it should.
