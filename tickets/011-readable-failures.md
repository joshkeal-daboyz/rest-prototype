# 011 · Make failures readable: say what broke and why

owner: ui (part A), renderer (parts B, E), interaction (part C), grid-simulator (part D; E depends on D)
status: open (awaiting G1)
branch: ticket/011-readable-failures

## Josh's words
On the battery run, after the fix: "it's still a hard read on why it breaks."

## Goal
When something breaks because of what the player did, the game says so in words, at the moment it happens, and the broken thing looks broken from across the map.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] **A (ui): event notices.** A short notice near the top of the map, in words, for each of these events, fading after ~4 s and stacking if several arrive: `fuse:blown` → *Fuse blew: 3 load through a ≤1 fuse* (numbers from the payload); `grid:brownout` entering → *Brownout: load 6 over capacity 4*; `battery:flat` (not stopped) → *Battery flat*; a refused relay throw → *No power at that relay*. The notice text logic is pure and tested. Notices are text, not colour.
- [ ] **B (renderer): a blown fuse looks blown, and load is shown, not written.** A readable `blown-fuse` draws the fuse bar snapped in two with a scorch mark and the `≤rating` label in the alarm colour, large enough to read at map scale; the pop on blow grows to ~2× its current size. **Lamp load:** Josh's words after playing: "the 3 is off to the side, I didn't understand that the lamp required additional power." So a lamp's load is drawn as **size and count**, not a bare digit: a load-1 lamp is the standard circle; a load-3 lamp is drawn as a cluster of three bulbs (or one lamp three times the area) with a `load 3` caption under it, so it visibly *eats more* than its neighbours. **Brightness scales with load** (Josh's words: "Objects that require more energy should be 2-3x brighter"): a lit load-3 lamp glows 2–3× as bright as a load-1 lamp — a larger, more intense light pool and a hotter core — and the same scale applies to its dark state (a bigger, dimmer disc), so the hungry lamp reads as hungry whether on or off. Tune the curve so load 1 → 1×, load 2 → 2×, load 3 → 3× in pool radius/alpha, capped so a load-5 lamp doesn't wash out its neighbours. The feed's readout and the fuse's `≤1` use the same unit word (`load`), so `≤1` reads as "carries load 1". Josh checks that the mill reads as hungry at a glance.
- [ ] **B1 (renderer): a dead junction shows its cost.** Josh on behind-the-first: "why do you need two wires to fix the last leg … it's not clear." A readable `dead-junction` is drawn as a junction box with **two broken stubs** (two gaps, one on each side, each with a small spark/char mark), so the two splices it needs are visible before the player clicks; a healthy junction stays the plain dot. The hover prompt (part C) reads *Dead junction — needs 2 wires* / *Repair (2 wires)*. The same idea applies to any fault whose part count is more than one: draw one gap per part.
- [ ] **B2 (renderer, from ticket 007):** the map fit leaves room for the HUD (top inset from `main.ts`), so the mill isn't half hidden under the parts chips. Fold ticket 007 part A in here.
- [ ] **D (grid-simulator): name the culprit.** Josh's words: "when a fuse blows it should send a shock animation along the line to the item that was last added to the line, then the fuse should blow and sparks should fly out." So `fuse:blown` gains `culprit: string` — the lamp id that was newly powered through this fuse by the action that caused the blow (the lamp in its downstream load now that was not there before the action; if several, the one with the largest load) — and `path: readonly string[]` — the line ids from the fuse to that lamp, in order. Tested on an inline map and on `battery-run` (breaker first → culprit `mill`). Pure; the event payload type is added to `src/core/events.ts` by the lead-architect.
- [ ] **E (renderer): the shock, then the blow.** `main.ts` forwards `fuse:blown` into `renderer.notify(event)` (a new method; the renderer stays bus-free). On it, the renderer plays a sequence: a bright shock pulse travels along `path` from the fuse to the culprit (~0.4 s, a hot white-blue streak with a crackle of sparks at the culprit), then the fuse pops with sparks flying out in all directions (~0.6 s, bigger than today's pop), and only then settles into the broken-fuse look from B. The simulator's state is already "blown" during the animation; the renderer keeps drawing the fuse intact until the pop lands, so the story reads in order. Sequence timing is a pure, tested module; no allocations per frame (preallocate the sequence slots). Josh checks the feel.
- [ ] **C (interaction): hover explains.** Hovering a fusebox shows *Fuse ≤1 — carrying 3* (current load through it, from a new `GridState.fuseLoad: ReadonlyMap<string, number>` if the grid-simulator adds it in a one-line sub-ticket; otherwise *Fuse ≤1*) or *Blown fuse — Repair (fuse)* / *Blown fuse — Needs: fuse*. Hovering a lamp shows *Lamp, load 3*. Tests for the prompt strings.
